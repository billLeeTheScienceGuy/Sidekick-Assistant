# Summaries and calendar handoff (0.25)

The Summary tab derives a daily overview from saved events and tasks. Plans,
information-only events and current history states stay separate. Undated saved
items appear in their own section rather than being assigned to today. Drafts and
dismissed items are excluded. Overnight activities overlap the relevant days;
day boundaries use the phone timezone, including 23/25-hour DST days. A reminder
today may refer to a later activity, so both timestamps are shown explicitly.

Choose a day using Previous/Today/Next or the calendar picker. View or edit opens
the existing editor; edits and deletions are reflected in the derived summary.
No separate summary database or stale generated copy is created. History shows
current Done/Cancelled status, not an invented completion or cancellation date.

Recent conversation highlights are bounded, exact source excerpts selected from
temporary live text. They retain corrections rather than inventing a narrative
or attributing all statements to the user. Clearing recent conversation removes
the excerpts while preserving saved events. Stop listening before clearing an
active conversation. This is an extractive digest, not general LLM summarization.

An active saved event with a known start can open the installed calendar editor.
The user reviews and saves it there. Only title, known times, timezone and venue
are sent; transcript/evidence is not exported. Unknown end times are omitted;
the calendar editor may supply its own default for the user to review. There is
no background calendar write, account selection, synchronization or duplicate
tracking. Information-only events can also be handed off explicitly.

English named attendance statements can update one matching event across several
activities. Pronoun attendance requires a single unambiguous activity. Bounded
affirmative/negative phrases also work in Korean, Chinese, French, German,
Russian and Arabic, subject to user attribution and reported/quoted-speech guards.
Withdrawal retains an informational event and removes its reminder. A later
rejoining statement restores personal attendance. These phrases remain a small
grammar; they do not establish speech or speaker-recognition accuracy.
